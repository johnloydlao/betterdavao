# Access Disaster Preparedness Information and Early Warning Systems — Davao City

The **City Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Office (CDRRMO)** publishes advisories and runs the city's early warning systems.

---

## 1) Where to Get Alerts and Information

- **CDRRMO Facebook page:** [facebook.com/DavaoDRRMO](https://www.facebook.com/DavaoDRRMO) posts **real-time weather advisories**.
- **Davao City Disaster Radio:** the city-owned radio station used for CDRRMO announcements and for disaster-related interviews with city officials. Phone (082) 298-8781.
- **Your barangay:** barangays relay warnings and calls for evacuation to residents.
- **CDRRMO website:** cdrrmo.davaocity.gov.ph.

---

## 2) Early Warning Systems

- **Sirens:** the city has installed sirens in the barangays. After the first citywide drill in 2017, when only six or seven sirens were working, the city increased the number. A siren is also used to start a pre-emptive evacuation for river overflow.
- **Coastal cameras:** the CDRRMO monitors the sea with coastal cameras to watch for a tsunami, such as a receding shoreline, so no one has to wait and watch.
- **Communications van:** the city has a communications vehicle with a hydraulic tower and antenna, so the CDRRMO can keep communicating if the usual facilities fail.
- **Drills:** the annual Shake Out drill and the Riverwide Simex practice these systems. See [Join disaster drills](/join-disaster-drills-and-evacuation-planning).

---

## 3) What to Do When You Hear a Warning

- Do not panic. Follow your barangay's instructions and call for evacuation.
- **Earthquake:** duck, cover and hold, then move to your safe zone.
- **Tsunami or river overflow:** evacuate to your barangay's safe zone or evacuation center.

---

## 4) Prepare Ahead

- Ask your Barangay Hall for your **safe zone** and evacuation center.
- Save the emergency numbers in [Emergency response procedures](/learn-about-emergency-response-procedures-and-evacuation-routes).
- Join your barangay's drills.
- Schools can ask the CDRRMO for preparedness activities for their students.

The city's published pages do not describe a mobile-phone text alert service, so rely on the sources above.
